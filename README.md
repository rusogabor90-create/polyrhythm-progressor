# MetronomeCanvas 🎼🖌️

**Precision temporal mapping for composers, session players, and audio-visual artists.**
*Where rhythm becomes a visual language.*

---

## Why MetronomeCanvas? 🤔

Every musician has felt the tyranny of the metronome. The cold, mechanical ticking that measures time but ignores *feeling*. MetronomeCanvas reimagines this relationship. Instead of a sterile click-track, we offer a **visual time-map** that transforms pulse into pattern, accent into architecture, and tempo into terrain.

This is not a practice tool. It is a **sonic drafting table**—a space where the grid of rhythm becomes a canvas for expression.

---

## The Philosophy 🧭

Think of standard metronomes as **calculators**—they give you the answer, but they don't show their work. MetronomeCanvas is the **graph paper** of the musical world. It doesn't just tell you *when* to play; it shows you *where* you are in the phrase, how much time has passed, and where the next beat lives in space.

This visual dimension allows for:
- **Subdivision awareness** beyond simple counting
- **Polyrhythmic clarity** without cognitive overload
- **Dynamic accent mapping** for syncopation exploration
- **Muscle memory acceleration** through multisensory feedback

---

## Core Features 🌟

### 1. Interactive Pulse Grid
Every beat, division, and subdivision renders as a **spatial element** on an infinite canvas. As the tempo flows, these elements pulse with intensity proportional to their dynamic weight. You don't hear the rhythm—you *see* it breathe.

### 2. Gesture-Responsive Tempo Tracking
Use your device's motion sensors or mouse movement to subtly push and pull the tempo. This isn't a gimmick; it simulates the **natural rubato of human performance** while maintaining a structural anchor for your practice.

### 3. Harmonic Syncopation Engine
Input a chord progression or scale. The engine suggests rhythmic patterns that **interlock with the harmonic rhythm**—not just metrical time. This bridges the gap between groove and harmony in ways traditional metronomes never attempt.

### 4. Practice Session Recorder
Capture 60-second loops of your playing alongside the visual grid. Overlay them to compare your **implied time** against the reference. The system highlights `attack drift` and `release deviation` with color-coded arcs.

### 5. Accessibility-First Interface
We've designed every control with **mobility and cognitive variety** in mind:
- High-contrast themes (including dark-mode, ink-paper, and braille-friendly palettes)
- Keyboard-only workflow (no mouse required for any operation)
- Screen-reader announcements for tempo changes and subdivision changes
- Single-switch scanning for those use alternative input devices

### 6. Multilingual Time Language
Switch between **English, German, Japanese, Hindi, and Swahili** for all terminology related to rhythm theory. This ensures that the platform accommodates musicians from every part of the globe without losing musical nuance.

### 7. 24/7 Tempo Concierge Support
Are you stuck at 3 AM on a tricky odd-meter passage? Our support team operates on a **round-the-world shift pattern**. Human help is available via in-app chat, never a bot that guesses your intent. Yes—even at 04:30 on a Sunday, there is a real percussionist with a background in music theory ready to assist.

## Visual Language 🎨

The interface uses **heat-map color theory**:
- **Cool blues** (just beginning a phrase)
- **Teal greens** (steady-state groove)
- **Amber yellows** (approaching an accent)
- **Deep reds** (the accent lands or is about to be shaped)

This gradient is applied not just to the beats, but to the **spacing bars between them**, giving a continuous visual flow rather than discrete blocks.

### Desktop Experience

## The Common Workflow 🧩

1. **Set your base tempo** and select a subdivision (triplets, sixteenths, quintuplets, etc.)
2. **Draw your accent patterns** directly on the grid
3. **Enable the Harmonizer** to sync accent placement with chord tones
4. **Practice with visual guides** for 10 minutes
5. **Record your last pattern** and review the overlay

The visual memory of the grid stays with you long after you close the browser tab. This is the **ultimate goal**—to train your internal clock to visualize time even without the screen.

## For Different Musicians 🎻

### For Classical Players
The **precision mode** visualizes sub-cents around the beat, helping string players understand where intonation and time meet. It reveals that rhythm, like pitch, has *color* and *weight*.

### For Electronic Producers
The **DAW-mimicry mode** displays patterns as MIDI-like piano-roll blocks, allowing you to draft drum patterns that follow a fluid pulse rather than a rigid grid.

### For Jazz Improvisers
The **oscillator overlay** syncs tonal brightness to offbeat placements, encouraging players to place notes *behind* or *in front* of the beat with expressive intent.

### For String Quartets
A **multi-user mode** allows four devices to sync. Each player sees their part highlighted in the shared rhythm, with a conductor-style global marker showing the composite pulse.

## Tech Notes ⚙️

MetronomeCanvas is built on **Web Audio API** with a custom event-loop scheduler that optimizes visual refresh rate independent of audio latency. The entire app is **client-side rendered** with localStorage persistence for your settings and patterns. There are no advertising trackers, analytics scripts, or data-collection modules. Your practice data remains on your device.

We support the latest two major versions of Chrome, Firefox, Safari, and Edge, as well as any browser that successfully passes the **Web Audio API conformance test** in 2026.

## Frequently Asked Philosophies 🤷

**Q: Does this replace my traditional metronome?**
A: It replaces the *situation* where a metronome feels like a barrier. It doesn't replace the ticking sound—you can still enable a classic click. But now you have the option to feel time spatially.

**Q: Can I sync it to my hardware MIDI devices?**
A: The 2026 roadmap includes a WebMIDI bridge for external synchronization. Currently, the app runs standalone.

**Q: Is there a way to export my grids into music notation software?**
A: Not in the current version. We are exploring a MIDI export format for rhythmic blocks only (duration and velocity mapped to the notes).
